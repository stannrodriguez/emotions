3bLeaf page — spelled-out coordinates, candor not alarm
ANGRY / RESENTFUL unpleasant · simmering

Resentful

Anger that has settled in around a specific unfairness — usually one you keep re-encountering, and often aimed at someone you haven't confronted. It accumulates: each new instance lands on the pile of the old ones.

If this word fits, the naming has already started to work: labeling an emotion precisely measurably lowers its intensity.

NEARBY

bitter — resentment that has stopped expecting repair. Resentment still holds a live claim; bitterness has filed it away as how things are.

indignant — hotter and more public. Indignation is anger at unfairness on principle, often on someone else's behalf; resentment keeps a personal ledger.

WHAT HELPS

Name the cost

Write the sentence “I'm resentful because X keeps costing me Y.” Be exact about X and Y. Precision turns a mood into a claim you can check against the facts.

WHERE IT BREAKS — if the sentence stops changing from day to day, writing has become rehearsal. Stop refining the grievance and act on it.

Reappraise the story

Look for the least-damning explanation that still fits every fact — “they're overloaded,” not “they don't respect me.” If it genuinely fits, the resentment loses its target.

WHERE IT BREAKS — misused, reappraisal rationalizes an arrangement you should renegotiate or leave. If the facts really are unfair, no better story fixes that.

Make the request

Resentment usually marks an unspoken contract someone keeps breaking. Say the term out loud to the person who can change it: one sentence, no case file.

WHERE IT BREAKS — only works where someone can actually grant it. If you've asked twice and nothing moved, the next step is a consequence, not a third ask.
